Probability of constraint satisfaction and Robust Optimization

This post explores some of the properties of robust mean-variance optimization.

Consider the classic mean-variance optimization model to select a portfolio x∈RN\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^Nx∈RN

min⁡xx⊺Qxs.t. 1⊺x=1, x≥0, μ⊺x≥rmin, \min_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{x}^{\intercal} Q \mathbf{x} \quad \textit{s.t.}\quad \mathbf{1}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x} = 1, \ \mathbf{x} \geq \mathbf{0},\ \boldsymbol{\mu}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x} \geq r_{\text{min}}, xmin​x⊺Qxs.t. 1⊺x=1, x≥0, μ⊺x≥rmin​,

where rminr_{\text{min}}rmin​ is the target return, Q\mathbf{Q}Q is the covariance matrix of asset returns, and μ\boldsymbol{\mu}μ is the expected return.

We do not know the true values of μ\boldsymbol{\mu}μ and Q\mathbf{Q}Q. Instead, we only have data to estimate these quantities. We form the corresponding estimates μ^\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}μ^​ and Q^\hat{\mathbf{Q}}Q^​ from a dataset of returns S={r(i)}i=1TS = \{\mathbf{r}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^TS={r(i)}i=1T​ and solve the MVO model with μ\boldsymbol{\mu}μ and Q\mathbf{Q}Q replaced with μ^\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}μ^​ and Q^\hat{\mathbf{Q}}Q^​. Let xMVO(μ^,Q^)\mathbf{x}_{\text{MVO}} \left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}, \hat{\mathbf{Q}} \right)xMVO​(μ^​,Q^​) denote an MVO solution obtained using estimates μ^\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}μ^​ and Q^\hat{\mathbf{Q}}Q^​.

What is random here? The estimates μ^\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}μ^​ and Q^\hat{\mathbf{Q}}Q^​ are random, since they depend on a random sample SSS. We can then ask, "What is the probability of meeting the constraints?"

PS[μ⊺xMVO(μ,Q)≥rmin]=1\mathbb{P}_S[\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\intercal} \mathbf{x}_{\text{MVO}} \left(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \mathbf{Q} \right) \geq r_{\text{min}}] = 1PS​[μ⊺xMVO​(μ,Q)≥rmin​]=1 since xMVO\mathbf{x}_{\text{MVO}}xMVO​ is a deterministic quantity and satisfies the return constraint by definition.

Similarly, one can consider the probability of meeting the return constraint when using the estimates: P[μ⊺xMVO(μ^,Q^)≥rmin]=?\mathbb{P}[\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\intercal} \mathbf{x}_{\text{MVO}} \left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}, \hat{\mathbf{Q}} \right) \geq r_{\text{min}}] = ? P[μ⊺xMVO​(μ^​,Q^​)≥rmin​]=?. Unfortunately, this probability does not equal 1. This drawback motivates the use of robust optimization, where we replace the constraint
